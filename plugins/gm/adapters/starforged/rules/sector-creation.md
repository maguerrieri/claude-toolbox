# Build a Starting Sector

*Adapted from* Ironsworn: Starforged Rulebook, *chapter 2, "Build a Starting Sector" (pp. 114–127), © 2022 Shawn Tomkin, licensed [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/). Abridged for solo play with this adapter: the book's page references now point at this adapter's tables, and its worksheet and group-play notes are left out. This file is licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (see `SOURCE.md`).*

A sector is "a settled or explored area of space" that serves as the backdrop as your adventures begin (the book allows about 30–45 minutes). The exercise is optional: "If you want to play in a more free-form galaxy, you can also handwave or ignore sectors entirely." "Keep things moving, and don't get hung up on details."

Record what you make in the campaign: settlements, planets and stars in `locations.md`, the connection in `npcs.md`, the sector trouble in `threads.md`, and the sector's name, region and map notes under `campaign.md`'s truths.

## 1. Choose your starting region (p. 116)

- **Terminus:** "Settlements are relatively common here, and spaceborne routes between communities are usually well-charted." Start here to focus on people and communities.
- **Outlands:** "This region represents an area of recent expansion. Settlements here are scattered, and navigational paths are often uncharted and perilous." Start here for a wild frontier.
- **Expanse:** "Only a few bold pioneers have delved these far-flung reaches." Start here for lonely exploration of uncharted space.
- **Void:** the reaches beyond the Forge. "Don't start your campaign here."

The region picks the column for the region-specific tables below (`terminus`, `outlands`, `expanse`).

## 2. Determine the number of settlements (p. 116)

| Region | Settlements |
|---|---|
| Terminus | 4 |
| Outlands | 3 |
| Expanse | 2 |

"Note that this doesn't represent a standard for how you'll populate other sectors as you explore the Forge."

## 3. Generate settlement details (p. 117)

For each settlement, roll on:

- Settlement Name: `oracles/settlement/name.md`
- Location: `oracles/settlement/location.md`
- Population: `oracles/settlement/population/<region>.md`
- Authority: `oracles/settlement/authority.md`
- Settlement Projects: `oracles/settlement/projects.md`; roll 1–2 times

"Keep it short and simple. For now, don't roll on the other settlement tables."

## 4. Generate planets (p. 118)

For each planetside or orbital settlement, roll the planet's type once on `oracles/planet/class.md`. Optionally name the planet (the sample names for each type are `oracles/planet/<type>/name.md`). "For now, don't roll on the other tables available for each planet."

## 5. Generate stars (optional, p. 118)

Roll once per settlement on `oracles/space/stellar-object.md`. "An unusual star such as a corrupted star or impending supernova might actually have a narrative impact on your starting situation."

## 6. Create a sector map (p. 119)

"Keep it simple." Arrange the settlements however you like. Distances are abstract: the map shows only whether places are clustered or remote. A map is "absolutely not required"; in a text campaign, a line in `locations.md` on each settlement's place in the sector is enough.

## 7. Create passages (p. 120)

"A passage is a charted route among the drifts." Following one, you Set a Course (one roll); off a passage, you Undertake an Expedition, and a successful expedition charts a new passage. Starting passages:

| Region | Passages |
|---|---|
| Terminus | 3 |
| Outlands | 2 |
| Expanse | 1 |

"Each passage must do one of two things: Connect two settlements. Connect a settlement to the edge of your sector map (leading to another sector)." A settlement no passage reaches needs an expedition to trailblaze a route.

## 8. Zoom in on a settlement (p. 122)

Choose the most interesting settlement (perhaps where your story opens) and roll on:

- First Look: `oracles/settlement/first-look.md`; roll 1–2 times
- Settlement Trouble: `oracles/settlement/trouble.md`

Follow any result that points at another table (`→ Roll …`). If the settlement is planetside or in orbit, also roll that planet type's tables (`oracles/planet/<type>/`): atmosphere; observed from space (1–2 times); planetside feature (1–2 times); for a Vital World, also diversity and biomes. "Don't roll on the planet's settlement table": assume one inhabited settlement for now.

## 9. Create a local connection (p. 123)

You start with a single connection in this sector, made with the Make a Connection move (`rules/moves.md`): "Instead of rolling, assume an automatic strong hit. This connection is already established as you begin your campaign, so no roll is necessary."

- **Role:** their duty, expertise or background. Specific beats general ("xenoarchaeologist is more inspiring than researcher"). Connections aren't shipmates or companions: they're independent characters whose lives sometimes cross yours. Leave it to fate with `oracles/character/role.md`.
- **Rank:** troublesome, dangerous, formidable, extreme or epic; "for a starting connection, consider making them troublesome or dangerous." Rank is the challenge of the relationship, not the person's nature.
- **Characteristics:** envision them, or roll `oracles/character/first-look.md` (1–2 times), `oracles/character/goal.md`, and `oracles/character/revealed-aspect.md` (1–2 times).
- **Home:** a settlement in this sector; the one from step 8 gets them involved right away.
- **Name:** a name or callsign; for inspiration, `oracles/character/name.md`.

"Whenever your connection aids you on a move closely associated with their role, you may add +1 and take +1 momentum on a hit." Record them as a connection on the character sheet (a progress track at their rank) and in `npcs.md`.

## 10. Introduce a sector trouble (p. 126)

"You've heard rumors of a sector-wide peril, conflict, or mystery." Come up with one, roll `oracles/action.md` + `oracles/theme.md` for inspiration, or pick or roll on `oracles/campaign-launch/sector-trouble.md`. Envision how it might manifest.

## 11. Finalize the starting sector (p. 126)

Name the sector, or roll `oracles/space/sector-name.md` (or its prefix and suffix on their own, `oracles/space/sector-name/`). Set a controlling power if a faction came to the forefront in your truths; if not, keep it ambiguous ("Sectors can be isolated, chaotic, or home to many insignificant factions"). For faction details in play, use the faction oracles (`oracles/faction/`).

Next: begin your adventure (`rules/campaign-launch.md`, *Begin Your Adventure*).
