"Work through the exercises in this order" (p. 80):

1. **Choose your truths** (below, then `rules/truths.md`): decide what is true for your version of the Forge.
2. **Create your character** (`rules/character-creation.md`): background, abilities and characteristics.
3. **Build a starting sector** (`rules/sector-creation.md`): a location to explore at the start of your campaign.
4. **Begin your adventure** (below): face an inciting incident and swear an iron vow.

"You might find your creative batteries running low if you pack the exercises into a single session." Offer the player a break between them. Set content flags before you start: the Set a Flag move (`rules/moves.md`, Session Moves) is how the player marks lines and veils, at any point, and Change Your Fate is how they adjust flagged content when it comes up.

## Choose Your Truths (pp. 81–98)

**Default assumptions** (p. 81). "The following points are all generally true, and represent your default starting place":

- "This is a perilous future. Two centuries ago, your people fled a cataclysm and settled a distant galaxy they call the Forge. This is a chaotic place full of dangers and mysteries."
- "This is a lonely future. With some possible exceptions (that you'll identify as part of your own truths), humans are the only known intelligent life in this galaxy. Others once lived here, but only mysterious and perilous vaults remain to mark their legacy."
- "This is a diverse future. There is a vibrant mix of people and cultures among the humans of the Forge."
- "This is a far-flung future. Settlements lie scattered and often isolated from one another. Your starship can travel at faster-than-light speeds, but it's ponderously slow at a cosmic scale."
- "This is an unexplored future. Discoveries await. Even in settled regions, much of the Forge is unknown and uncharted."
- "This is a wondrous future. The Forge is a galaxy of ancient mysteries, spacefaring creatures, startling phenomenon, and other marvels."
- "This is a retro-future. Envision the technology you wield as only slightly advanced over today's real-world technologies—or even a step back in many ways. Resources are scarce, and the people of the Forge must cobble together what they can."
- "This is an unjust future. Those in power hoard resources, control technologies, and impose their will on others through force or cunning. Others must stand against these forces of imperialism and oppression."
- "This is a hopeful future. Despite these challenges, hope remains. Fulfilling your sworn vows is a realization of that hope."

**The 14 categories** are in `rules/truths.md`: Cataclysm, Exodus, Communities, Iron, Laws, Religion, Magic, Communication and Data, Medicine, Artificial Intelligence, War, Lifeforms, Precursors, Horrors.

**How to select a truth** (p. 82). For each category, either choose one of the three options, roll for one (`oracles/truths/<category>.md`), or customize one or craft your own. "A few truths also include subchoices in oracle tables. As with the main categories, you can choose, roll, or customize from among them."

**Tips** (p. 83):

- *Keep it moving.* Read each option's summary first; the details and quest starters are "further inspiration after you make a pick." Skip a category that isn't relevant; it can be decided later, when it comes up in play.
- *Leave unanswered questions.* "Create the broad outlines for your campaign, while leaving room for inspiration and surprises."
- *Resolve contradictions* by bending the truths to fit, or keep one: "You may even discover through play that a choice you make here—which reflects common knowledge—isn't the actual truth after all."
- *Start to envision your character.* Each category ends with a prompt about your character, and suggests assets in ALL CAPS. Note any that interest the player for character creation.

## Begin Your Adventure (pp. 128–134)

### 1. Envision an inciting incident (p. 128)

"An inciting incident is the problem at the beginning of a story that propels the protagonists into action." If none comes to mind, mine the earlier exercises: the selected truths and their quest starters; the character's paths, backstory and starship; the sector's settlements, connection, and trouble.

What makes a good one (p. 129): *make it personal*; *make it a problem that won't go away on its own*; *give it a ticking clock*; *up the stakes* if it seems mundane; and *limit the scope*: "Don't try to save the universe out of the gate. Envision a trouble or goal that seems manageable—something you can overcome in a session or two of play."

Still stuck (p. 130)? Roll `oracles/action.md` + `oracles/theme.md`, roll `oracles/character/goal.md`, or pick or roll on `oracles/campaign-launch/inciting-incident.md`. "Make it relevant to your character and worthy of a sworn vow."

### 2. Set the scene (p. 131)

Where does the story begin? Then start one of two ways:

- **A prologue:** play a scene or two of your character going about their business ("the sequence in a television show or movie before the credits roll"), then introduce the inciting incident when the time is right. With no inciting incident yet, use the prologue to find one in play.
- **In medias res:** "You start your story at a crucial point. Your inciting incident is happening now, or is an imminent threat."

### 3. Make the move (p. 132)

Make the **Swear an Iron Vow** move (`rules/moves.md`) in response to the inciting incident: an action roll, +heart, adding +1 if you swear it to your connection. Write the vow in your character's words. "Since this is a starting vow, hopefully dealt with in a session or two of playtime, give it a rank of troublesome or dangerous." Record it on the sheet and in `threads.md`.

### 4. Next steps (p. 134)

Envision what the move's outcome means and how you respond. "And then, play. Ask and answer questions about the situations, people, creatures, and places you encounter. When you're unsure, Ask the Oracle. When you do something or encounter something within the scope of a move, make that move."
