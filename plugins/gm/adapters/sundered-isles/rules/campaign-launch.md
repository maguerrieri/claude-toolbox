The book's recommended order of exercises (p. 70); change it if the player would rather, say, build the character before the setting:

1. **Select your realm** (below): the overall setting.
2. **Choose your truths** (below, then `rules/truths.md`): what is true in this version of the isles.
3. **Set your factions** (below): the powers at play.
4. **Create your character** (`rules/character-creation.md`, steps 1–6).
5. **Take command** (`rules/character-creation.md`, step 7): your ship and crew, if any, and a final asset.
6. **Chart your course** (`rules/sector-creation.md`): a map of your starting waters.
7. **Set the scene** (below): where the story begins, and the vow that starts it.

Prep is play: if an exercise feels like work, skip it or take a shortcut (p. 68). Running them all in one sitting can wear the player out, so offer breaks between them (p. 70). **Quick start** (p. 69): for a player who already has the setting in mind, do only character creation and Take Command, then play, and settle the rest as it comes up.

## Before you start (p. 68)

- **Tone.** Ask what adventures the player wants: swashbuckling, treasure hunting, rebellion against empire, intrigue at court, spooky exploration, eldritch horror, or a mix. The answers steer the choices below.
- **Build the world mindfully.** The isles can echo the real age of sail, which was an age of conquest and enslavement. Prioritize diversity; don't depict any people of the isles as monolithic, primitive or exotic; if empires exist, they are the enemy, and the character is meant to break their machine, not serve it; and slavery isn't assumed to exist. If the player wants it in the setting, flag it, and never trivialize or romanticize it.
- **Safety.** The player's lines and veils are the first flags of the Set a Flag move (`rules/moves.md`, Session Moves), and Change Your Fate adjusts flagged content when it comes up. Flags can be set at any time, with no explanation needed.

## Select your realm (pp. 71–77)

Choose one and record it under `campaign.md`'s truths:

- **Seafaring** (the default): the isles lie within vast, perilous, equatorial seas. Every oracle, asset and truth in this adapter assumes it.
- **Skyfaring**: the isles are the airborne shards of a shattered continent, sailed by airships among storms and shifting winds. Reimagine the oracles to fit: an island is a floating fragment, a sea battle is fought in three dimensions.
- **Starfaring**: the isles are worlds adrift on aetherial currents, sailed by aether ships. Starforged's oracles (planets, space encounters, creatures, derelicts, precursor vaults) join the Sundered Isles ones, and technological Starforged assets can be re-themed as aether-powered. Chart Your Course gives way to Starforged's sector creation (`rules/sector-creation.md` says how).

Outside the seafaring realm, "sea" and "island" become figures of speech, and the abstract oracles (Action, Theme, Descriptor, Focus) serve better than the setting-specific ones (p. 115).

## Choose your truths (pp. 78–89)

The 11 categories are in `rules/truths.md`: Sundering, Relics, Modern Era, Iron Vows, Navigation, Empires, Piracy, Religion, Magic, Beasts, Horrors.

For each category, the player **chooses** one of the three options, **rolls** for one (`oracles/truths/<category>.md`), or **customizes** one or writes their own. To keep things moving, read just each option's one-line summary; the details and quest starters are there for inspiration. Each category ends with suggested assets for the character and factions for the setting: note any the player likes for the next two exercises. (The Starforged rulebook's tips on truths, p. 83, apply too: skip what isn't relevant yet, leave questions open, and let play reveal that a "truth" was only common belief.)

If the truths bring curses, the supernatural or strange technologies into the isles, note that the cursed die is in play (`adapter.md`, *The cursed die*).

## Set your factions (pp. 90–91)

Choose or envision **three or four** influential factions, taking the lead from the truths. Record each under `campaign.md`'s truths as a name, its type (society, organization, empire or cursed) and two to four commonly known facts, or just a placeholder ("Tyrannical Empire").

- **Inspiration:** roll or pick on `oracles/adventures/factions/societies.md`, `oracles/adventures/factions/organizations.md`, `oracles/adventures/factions/empires.md` and `oracles/adventures/factions/cursed.md` (pp. 54–57), or build one with the faction oracles (`oracles/faction/`, p. 190). If empires have no place in these isles, ignore them; if the supernatural doesn't exist, cursed factions are only rumors.
- **The relevance test.** For each faction: does it fit this world? Can it drive quests, connections or threats for the character? Can it clash or scheme with at least one other faction? A "no" means rethink it; a tentative "yes" is enough.
- **Relationships.** Give each faction a relationship with at least one other. Where it isn't obvious, roll `oracles/faction/relationship.md` for one toward another, and adjust or reroll a result that doesn't fit. These are common knowledge, and the truth may differ.
- **Ties to the character.** Note any faction that offers the character an origin, a backstory or an enemy.

## Set the scene (pp. 110–111)

### The status quo

Where is the character as the story opens: aboard ship, on the road inland, arriving at a settlement, meeting the local connection? Use the map from Chart Your Course. Start with a **prologue** (a scene or two of everyday life before the trouble arrives) or **in medias res** (the trouble is happening now).

### The inciting incident

Near the start, the character will Swear an Iron Vow to a pressing goal of limited scope, troublesome or dangerous. If none comes to mind, mine the earlier exercises:

- **Truths:** a situation, conflict or danger, or a quest starter worth pursuing.
- **Factions:** a mission for an ally, or a blow against an enemy.
- **Character:** a duty, skill or goal from the paths; a danger or goal in the backstory.
- **Command:** something about the ship's nature or history that must be dealt with; a crew in need, in hardship or under a curse; or, with no ship, a quest to win one.
- **Local area:** a settlement's troubles or projects, a connection who needs something, a location that demands investigation.

Still stuck? Roll for it: a patron's task from `oracles/action.md` + `oracles/theme.md`, an incident at sea from `oracles/seafaring/peril.md`, a personal mission from `oracles/character/goal.md`, or a treasure map from `oracles/treasure/location.md`. Or just make something happen: marauders attack, a tempting prize appears on the horizon, a dying mariner hands over a bloodstained map. The Starforged rulebook's advice on a good inciting incident (p. 129) holds: make it personal, make it a problem that won't go away on its own, and keep its scope small.

### Make the move

Make the **Swear an Iron Vow** move (`rules/moves.md`) in response to the incident, and write the vow in the character's words, ranked troublesome or dangerous. Record it on the sheet and in `threads.md`. Then play: ask and answer questions, Ask the Oracle when unsure, and make a move when the fiction calls for one.

These exercises are a foundation, not a mandate (p. 111): keep what works, and change what doesn't, even once play has begun.
