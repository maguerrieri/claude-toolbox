The book's campaign setup (summary, p. 202):

1. **Create your character** (`rules/character-creation.md`): concept, name, stats, assets, background bonds and background vow.
2. **Create your world** (below): your truths, and where your story begins.
3. **Begin your adventure** (below): envision an inciting incident, set the scene, and Swear an Iron Vow.

Either of the first two can come first (p. 31): start with the character and build the world around them, or start with the world for inspiration. The two can also be worked together, since a truth can limit the character's options (p. 193).

## Create Your World (p. 194)

### Your truths (p. 122)

For each of the 11 categories in `rules/truths.md` (The Old World, Iron, Legacies, Communities, Leaders, Defense, Mysticism, Religion, Firstborn, Beasts, Horrors), choose one of its three options, roll for one (`oracles/truths/<truth>.md`), or make your own truth. The book's guidance:

- Contradictions between choices are fine: bend an option to fit the others. You can even choose more than one option in a category. "There are no rules here."
- Your choices can limit or theme your character's assets: if magic is rare or unknown, rituals can be ignored, or themed as superstition and subtlety.
- Don't get mired in detail before play. Leave room for inspiration and surprises; some of what you pick reflects only what the people believe, and may turn out not to be the truth.
- Watch the quest starters: one may suggest a background vow or an inciting incident.

If the player would rather jump in, settle a few basic facts and discover the rest in play (p. 194).

### Where your story begins (p. 194)

Choose where your story begins. Look at the nine regions in `rules/regions.md` (or roll `oracles/place/region.md`): is the character part of a community, or wandering the wilderness? Envision the place, and write it to `locations.md`. To name a home settlement, roll `oracles/settlement/name.md`, as the book's example does.

## Begin Your Adventure (pp. 196–200)

### 1. Envision your inciting incident (p. 196)

An inciting incident is the problem at the start of the story that propels the character into action: the tipping point that forces them into a life of perilous quests. Envision an imminent threat or compelling need. For inspiration, use a quest starter (from `rules/truths.md`, `rules/regions.md` or `rules/encounters.md`), or Ask the Oracle (`rules/moves.md`; for a prompt, `oracles/action.md` + `oracles/theme.md`).

What makes a good one (pp. 196–197):

- **Make it personal.** Connect it to the character's background and motivations.
- **Make it a problem that won't go away on its own.** Its threats see it through even if ignored.
- **Give it a ticking clock.** If the character doesn't act, or is delayed, things get worse.
- **Make it universal.** Playing solo, make it a threat to other people the character cares about.
- **Up the stakes** with complications that make it more dire for the character, their family or their community.
- **Limit the scope.** Unlike the background vow, it's a problem to deal with now, and might be resolved in a session or two.

### 2. Set the scene (p. 198)

Start one of two ways:

- **The normal world:** a prologue that shows who the character is and how they live, with the inciting incident arriving during the first session. With no inciting incident yet, use the prologue to find one in play: ask questions, Ask the Oracle, and look for a chance to trigger a vow.
- **In media res:** start at a crucial point, with the inciting incident happening now or already known. Begin with a tense scene as the problem hits, or frame the scene where the character swears the vow.

### 3. Swear an Iron Vow (p. 199)

Make the **Swear an Iron Vow** move (`rules/moves.md`) in response to the inciting incident: an action roll, +heart, adding +1 if the vow is made to a person or community the character shares a bond with. Start with the fiction: envision the scene, what the character says and how they perform the ceremony. For an inciting incident, give the vow a rank of **troublesome, dangerous, or formidable**. Record it on the sheet and in `threads.md`.

### 4. Next steps (p. 200)

Interpret the move's result: a strong hit makes the path clear (the character might Undertake a Journey, or Compel someone into action); a weak hit leaves more questions than answers (perhaps Gather Information); a miss puts an obstacle in the way. Not sure where to begin? Ask the Oracle.

Optionally, sketch a **quest outline**: the journeys, enemies, information and help the quest might need, each major step a chance to Reach a Milestone. Treat it as a sketchy, unreliable plan, and drop it when the story goes elsewhere: "play to see what happens."
