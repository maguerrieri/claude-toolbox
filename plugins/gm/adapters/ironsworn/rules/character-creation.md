# Create Your Character

*Adapted from the* Ironsworn Rulebook, *chapter 2, "Your Character" (pp. 31–47), and chapter 7, "Starting Your Campaign" (pp. 193–196), © 2018 Shawn Tomkin, licensed [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/). Abridged for solo play with this adapter: the book's page references now point at this adapter's files, and its co-op and guided-play notes are left out. The steps follow the book's character creation summary (p. 47), which can be worked "in whatever order you prefer": choosing assets first and letting them guide the concept works well (p. 44). The character sheet is `sheet-template.md`; the asset cards are in `rules/assets/` (index: `rules/assets.md`). Where a step says to choose or roll, the player chooses; roll the named table when they say "surprise me" or want inspiration.

## 1. Envision your character (p. 31)

Consider their motivations, interests, skills, personality and weaknesses. One or two ideas about their background and goals are enough; the rest is fleshed out in play.

- **Be awesome.** The character is highly competent: smart, brave, driven, able to hold their own in a fight. They will still face hardship, make bad decisions and fail, and pushing on through failure is what makes them heroic.
- **Be who you want.** The people of the Ironlands are diverse, and a character is unbound by geography, lineage, sexual orientation and gender. The default setting is human-centric, with no rules for fantasy races, but it can be adjusted.

## 2. Choose a name (p. 33)

For inspiration, roll `oracles/name/ironlander/a.md` or `oracles/name/ironlander/b.md` (roll on either; surnames aren't used in the Ironlands).

## 3. Set your stats (p. 33)

Arrange 3, 2, 2, 1, 1 across **edge, heart, iron, shadow and wits**, in any order. The sheet describes each stat.

## 4. Set your health, spirit and supply (pp. 33–34)

Set each to **+5**. Supply is shared: the character and any allies use the same supply value while they travel together.

## 5. Set your momentum (p. 35)

Momentum **+2**, max momentum **+10**, momentum reset **+2**. Each marked debility lowers max momentum by 1; with one debility marked the reset is +1, and with more than one it's 0 (p. 36). All debilities start unmarked (p. 38).

## 6. Mark your background bonds (pp. 36, 195)

Give the character **up to three** bonds, each to a community or an individual, and mark one tick per bond on the bonds track. They don't need the Forge a Bond move. A bond can be held in reserve: when its person or community enters the story, mark it then and envision how it was formed.

## 7. Pick three assets (pp. 39–44)

Choose three assets from the four types: **companions** (`rules/assets/companion.md`), **paths** (`rules/assets/path.md`), **combat talents** (`rules/assets/combat-talent.md`) and **rituals** (`rules/assets/ritual.md`). Mix and match as fits the concept.

- **Requirements.** Some assets can only be taken once a narrative or mechanical requirement is met (p. 41). For example, Banner-Sworn needs a bond with a leader or faction, which a background bond can meet (p. 195).
- **Starting abilities.** A card's first ability is usually marked. If none is, choose one of its three to start (p. 42).
- **Companions** get a name, and an appearance and personality to envision (p. 39).
- **Combat talents** usually need a specific weapon at hand to use (p. 40).
- **Rituals** fit only a world with magic in it: if the truths make magic unknown, ignore the ritual assets, or theme them as superstition and subtlety (pp. 122, 193).

## 8. Note your equipment (p. 45)

Note any items with a narrative impact, and the equipment the assets rely on (a combat talent's weapon, say). Keep it simple. Supply covers mundane gear, rations and ammunition. By default, a fine sword or quality armor is rare and marks the character as someone of note; a typical Ironlander starts with cheap, mundane gear, and improving their lot can be part of the story.

## 9. Write your background vow (p. 195)

Envision a background vow to represent a primary motivation or ultimate goal: part of the character's backstory, sworn years ago or in response to some recent, major event. Give it a rank of **extreme or epic**, and write it on the sheet. It doesn't need the Swear an Iron Vow move. It may take months or years of story time to fulfill, or may simply color the character.

Experience starts unmarked (p. 45).
